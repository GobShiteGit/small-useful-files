How do I lay out a monthly habit tracker page?

Work out the usable area of the page, subtract the space for the title and column headers, and divide what is left by 31 rows. Then split the width into one day column and your habit columns. A little arithmetic up front stops the grid from running off the page.

Step by step

  1. Pick the trim size. For example, 6 in wide by 9 in tall.
  2. Set your margins. For example, 0.75 in inside, 0.5 in outside, 0.5 in top and bottom.
  3. Find the content area. Width: 6 - 0.75 - 0.5 = 4.75 in. Height: 9 - 0.5 - 0.5 = 8 in.
  4. Reserve space for the title and header row. For example, 0.4 in for "Month / Year" and 0.8 in for the habit name boxes.
  5. Divide the rest by 31. Remaining height: 8 - 0.4 - 0.8 = 6.8 in. 6.8 divided by 31 is about 0.22 in per row.
  6. Round down. Use 0.2 in per row. The 31 rows then take 6.2 in, and the whole page uses 0.4 + 0.8 + 6.2 = 7.4 in of the 8 in available. The spare 0.6 in is a safety buffer.
  7. Split the width. Give the day-number column 0.55 in. The remaining 4.2 in divided by 8 habit columns is 0.525 in each.

Why 31 rows

Months have between 28 and 31 days. If the page has 31 rows, one layout works for every month. The reader leaves the unused rows blank in shorter months.

Is the row height usable?

A row around 0.2 in tall is fine for a tick, a dot or an X. It is tight for writing words or numbers. Decide what people will do in each box. If they will only mark done or not done, small rows are fine. If they need to write, use fewer rows per page or fewer habits.

How many habit columns?

More columns means narrower boxes. With 8 columns on a 4.75 in wide area, each box is about half an inch wide. That works for marks. It leaves little room for habit names, so give the header row enough height for short labels, or leave the names for the reader to write in.

Check your work

  1. Add up the heights: title + header + (31 x row height). The total must be less than the content height.
  2. Add up the widths: day column + (habit columns x width). The total must not exceed the content width.
  3. Export to PDF and look at the last row. It should sit clearly above the bottom margin.
  4. Look at an odd page and an even page. The inside margin should be on opposite sides.

Common mistakes

  • Counting the margins twice, or forgetting the top and bottom margins altogether.
  • Rounding row height up, so the last row spills onto a new page.
  • Making header boxes so short that the reader cannot write a habit name.
  • Forgetting to check the page in the platform's previewer.
