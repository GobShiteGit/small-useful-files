What does "no bleed" mean for a paperback interior?

Bleed is artwork or color that runs past the edge of the page, so no thin white strip shows after the pages are trimmed. "No bleed" means nothing in your interior touches the page edge, so no extra area is needed.

Why bleed exists

Books are printed on larger sheets and then cut to size. The cut is never perfectly exact. If a shaded box or an image is meant to reach the edge, the file has to extend slightly beyond the trim line. Otherwise a small slip in cutting leaves a white sliver.

How much extra area you need is set by the platform. Check the current figure on the platform yourself rather than relying on a number from a guide.

When you can use no bleed

You can choose no bleed when everything on the page sits well inside the edge. That is typical for text-based interiors such as logs, planners, trackers and lined notebooks:

  • Black text and thin lines.
  • Boxes and grids that stop short of the edge.
  • A white background with no tint, border or image reaching the edge.

If your margins are around half an inch or more on the outer sides, nothing is near the edge.

When you need bleed

  • A full-page background color or gray tint.
  • A border that runs to the edge.
  • An image that touches the edge.
  • A header bar or tab that runs off the page.

If any page has one of these, you need to build the file with bleed and choose the bleed option when you upload.

How to decide, step by step

  1. Flip through every page of your PDF, not just the first few.
  2. Look for anything that touches or nearly touches the edge.
  3. If you find nothing, choose no bleed at upload. If you find something, either pull it back from the edge or switch to a bleed layout.
  4. Make sure the bleed setting you pick matches how the file was made. A file made without bleed should be uploaded as no bleed.
  5. Open the platform's previewer and check the edges.

A worked example with made-up numbers

You have a 6 in by 9 in page with a 0.5 in outside margin and a 0.5 in top and bottom margin. A grid fills the content area. The farthest the grid reaches toward the outside edge is 0.5 in from the paper edge. Nothing reaches the edge, so no bleed is fine.

Now you add a light gray bar across the top of each page that runs edge to edge. That bar touches the edge, so the file needs bleed. You would need to rebuild it, or remove the bar.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing bleed for a file that was not built with bleed. The sizes will not match and the platform may flag it.
  • Choosing no bleed for a file with edge-to-edge color.
  • Leaving a thin hairline border near the edge, which looks uneven if the cut shifts.
  • Not checking the whole book, since one page with an edge-touching element is enough to cause trouble.
