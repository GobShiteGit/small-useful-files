What is a gutter margin in a paperback interior?

The gutter margin, also called the inside or binding margin, is the blank space between your content and the spine edge of the page. It is usually made wider than the other margins because the pages curve into the binding and text close to the fold is harder to read.

Which side is the inside?

In a printed book, pages face each other in pairs. The spine sits between them.

  • Right-hand pages are the odd-numbered ones (1, 3, 5 and so on). The spine is on their left, so the inside margin is on the left.
  • Left-hand pages are the even-numbered ones (2, 4, 6 and so on). The spine is on their right, so the inside margin is on the right.

This is why one fixed left margin on every page is a common mistake. On every second page, the wide margin ends up on the outside edge, and the content sits too close to the fold.

A worked example with made-up numbers

Say your book is 6 in wide by 9 in tall. You choose an inside margin of 0.75 in and an outside margin of 0.5 in.

  1. Page width: 6 in.
  2. Subtract the inside margin: 6 - 0.75 = 5.25 in.
  3. Subtract the outside margin: 5.25 - 0.5 = 4.75 in.
  4. Your content width is 4.75 in on every page.

On page 3 (odd), the left margin is 0.75 in and the right is 0.5 in. On page 4 (even), the left is 0.5 in and the right is 0.75 in. The content block shifts a little from page to page, but its width stays the same.

How wide should it be?

There is no single answer. Two things matter:

  • Page count. A thicker book curls more at the spine, so it needs more room on the inside. A thin book needs less.
  • The platform's rules. Publishing platforms set minimum margins, often by page count and trim size. Those rules can change. Look up the current minimum for your exact trim size and page count on the platform itself before you build or upload anything.

A wider margin than the minimum gives you a safety buffer, but it also shrinks the writing area. For a tracker or journal where people write by hand near the spine, a little extra is usually worth it.

Common mistakes

  • Using the same left margin on all pages instead of mirroring it.
  • Putting a table, grid or ruled lines so close to the fold that the reader cannot see the first column when the book is open.
  • Setting the margin once for a 100-page book and then adding pages without checking the rules again.
  • Assuming last year's minimum still applies.

How to check your margins

  1. Open your PDF and look at one odd page and one even page.
  2. Measure or compare the space on the spine side against the outside edge. It should be larger on the spine side on both pages.
  3. Upload the file and use the platform's previewer. Look at the pages near the start, middle and end.
  4. If anything looks tight at the fold, widen the inside margin and re-export.
