How do I record a partial rent payment?

Record a partial payment against the specific charge it pays, with the date, amount and method, and then work out what is still owed on that charge. Do not just add it to a pile of money received.

Why a running total of money received is not enough

If you only note that a tenant paid 700 this month, you cannot tell later which month's rent it went toward or how much is still owed on each month. That gets awkward when a tenant pays in pieces for several months in a row. Matching each payment to one charge keeps the picture clear.

Steps

  1. Give every charge its own line. One line for each month's rent, and one for any other charge such as a fee or a repair bill. Each line has a short unique ID, for example `B-2026-11`, plus a due date and an amount.
  2. Record each payment on its own line. Write the date received, the amount, the method (cash, check, transfer) and the ID of the charge it pays.
  3. Add a note when it helps. For example: partial, rest due on the 20th.
  4. Work out the balance for that charge. Balance = charge amount minus the total of all payments matched to that charge.
  5. Note the date the balance reached zero. That date is the date the charge was fully paid, and you will want it if you ever calculate a late fee.

Worked example (made-up numbers)

Rent of 1200 is due on Nov 1. The charge ID is `B-2026-11`.

  • Nov 2: tenant pays 700 by transfer. Balance: 1200 - 700 = 500.
  • Nov 15: tenant pays 300 in cash. Balance: 500 - 300 = 200.
  • Nov 28: tenant pays 200 by transfer. Balance: 0. Fully paid on Nov 28.

Three payment lines, one charge line, and at any moment you can say exactly what is owed.

When one payment covers two charges

Say a tenant hands you 2000 and owes 1000 for October and 1000 for November. Split it into two payment lines of 1000, one for each charge ID. If the payment does not divide neatly, write down how you applied it. Your lease or local rules may say how payments must be applied, so check them yourself rather than guessing.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving out the charge ID. Without it you are back to guessing which month was paid.
  • Typing the tenant name slightly differently each time. Pick one spelling and keep it.
  • Reusing a charge ID. Each charge needs its own code.
  • Entering payments out of order. Record payments for a charge in date order so the running balance reads correctly.
  • Forgetting overpayments. If total payments exceed the charge, flag it so you can sort out the extra.

A note on rules

What you may or must do when a tenant pays only part of the rent depends on your lease and on where you live. This page covers record-keeping only. It is not legal advice, so check the current rules for your area yourself.
