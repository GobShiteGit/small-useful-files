How do I calculate a late fee on rent?

A common way to work out a rent late fee is: days late x daily fee, where the days only start counting after the grace period ends, and the total is limited by a cap if your lease sets one. Whether you may charge a fee at all, and how much, depends on your lease and where you live. Check both yourself before charging anything.

What you need from your lease

Before you do any arithmetic, find these four things:

  • Due date: usually the 1st of the month.
  • Grace days: how many days after the due date the tenant can pay with no fee.
  • Fee rule: a flat amount per day, a one-time flat amount, or a percentage. This page covers the per-day kind.
  • Cap: the most a single late rent can accrue, if the lease says so.

If your lease says something different from the steps below, follow your lease and the local rules, not this page.

Steps

  1. Add the grace days to the due date. That is the grace end date.
  2. Pick the end date for counting. If the rent is paid in full, use the date it was fully paid. If it is still unpaid, use today's date (or the date of the statement you are preparing).
  3. Subtract the grace end date from the end date. That is the days late. If the answer is zero or negative, there is no fee.
  4. Multiply days late by the daily fee.
  5. If there is a cap and your answer is higher, use the cap.
  6. Subtract any late fee you have already collected or chosen to waive. What is left is the late fee still outstanding.

Worked example (made-up numbers)

Rent of 1000 is due on October 1. The lease gives 5 grace days, a fee of 10 per day, and a cap of 100.

  • Grace end date: October 6.
  • The tenant pays 600 on October 10 and the remaining 400 on October 20. The rent is fully paid on October 20.
  • Days late: October 20 minus October 6 = 14.
  • Fee before cap: 14 x 10 = 140.
  • Cap applies: the fee is 100.

If the same rent were still unpaid on November 14, you would count to November 14 instead, and the cap would again limit the fee.

Decisions you must make clearly

  • Partial payments: the example above counts to the date the rent was fully paid. Some leases count differently, for example only on the unpaid part. Your lease decides.
  • No compounding: a late fee on top of an unpaid late fee is a different calculation. Do not do it unless your lease and local rules clearly allow it.
  • Waived fees: if you choose to waive a fee, write down that you did and the amount, so your records match what the tenant was told.

Common mistakes

  • Counting the grace days as late days.
  • Forgetting the cap, or applying it to the whole year instead of each late rent.
  • Counting from the wrong date because a payment was written down late.
  • Mixing up the amount charged and the amount collected.

Keep a record

Write down for each late rent: due date, grace end date, date fully paid, days late, fee calculated, fee collected or waived. Then you can explain any number to a tenant.
