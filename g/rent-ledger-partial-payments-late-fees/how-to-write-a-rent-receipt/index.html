How do I write a rent receipt?

A rent receipt is a short written proof that you received money from a tenant. It should show who paid, how much, on what date, for which rent period, by what method, and who received it. If the payment was partial, it should also show what is still owed. Some places have rules about receipts, especially for cash, so check the current rule where you are.

What to put on it

  • Receipt number or date: a simple running number helps you find it later.
  • Tenant name and the property or unit.
  • Amount received and the date received.
  • Payment method: cash, check (with the check number), or transfer.
  • What it covers: for example, "rent for November 2026", or "late fee for October".
  • Balance remaining, if any, for that period.
  • Name and signature of the person who received the money.

Steps

  1. Write down the payment the moment you receive it, with the date and amount.
  2. Decide what it covers. If it pays rent and a fee, list them on separate lines.
  3. Work out what is still owed for that period.
  4. Fill in the receipt and sign it.
  5. Give a copy to the tenant and keep a copy yourself.

Example (made-up numbers)

> Receipt no. 014 > Date received: November 20, 2026 > Received from: Sample Tenant A > Property: Unit 2 > Amount: 300.00 > Method: cash > For: part payment of rent for November 2026 > Rent for the month: 1200.00. Paid to date: 1000.00. Balance still owed: 200.00 > Received by: (name and signature)

The balance line protects both sides. The tenant sees what is left, and you have a record that they were told.

Cash needs extra care

A bank transfer or check leaves a trace. Cash does not. Always give a receipt for cash, and keep your copy. Whether a receipt is required, and what it must say, depends on where you are, so check the current local rule instead of assuming.

Partial payments

Do not write "paid in full" unless the month's balance is zero. Write the amount actually received and the balance. If you agreed to accept less or waive a fee, say so in plain words on the receipt.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving out the period the money is for.
  • Not keeping a copy.
  • Skipping the balance on a partial payment.
  • Mixing rent and late fees in one unclear amount.
  • Using different wording each time, which makes records hard to search.
