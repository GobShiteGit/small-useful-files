How do I record a partial rent payment?

Record a partial rent payment as its own entry, with the date, the amount, the payment method, and which month's rent it is for. Then subtract it from what was owed for that month to get the balance. Never overwrite the original rent amount. Keep the charge and the payments as separate lines.

Why separate lines matter

If you change the rent amount from 1200 to 500 when a tenant pays 700, you lose the record of what was owed. Later, if there is a disagreement, you cannot show what happened. Keeping the full charge and each payment separately gives you a trail.

Steps

  1. Write the charge first: the month, the due date, and the full amount owed.
  2. When money arrives, add a payment line: date received, amount, method (cash, check, transfer), and a short note.
  3. Mark which charge the payment is for. Do this at the time you receive it, not weeks later.
  4. Work out the balance: charge amount minus all payments for that charge so far.
  5. Add payments for the same charge in date order, oldest first, so the running total makes sense.
  6. When the balance reaches zero, write down the date it was fully paid. You may need that date for late fee calculations.

Worked example (made-up numbers)

Rent for November is 1200, due November 1.

  • November 2: tenant pays 700 by transfer. Note: "partial, rest due on the 20th". Balance: 1200 - 700 = 500.
  • November 20: tenant pays 300 in cash. Balance: 500 - 300 = 200.
  • November 28: tenant pays 200. Balance: 0. Fully paid on November 28.

Anyone reading these lines can see what was owed, what came in, and when the rent was cleared.

When one payment covers two months

If a tenant hands you 2000 for two months of 1000 rent, record it as two lines of 1000, one for each month. A single line that mixes months makes each month's balance hard to read.

When the payment is more than the charge

If the payment is larger than what is owed for that month, do not hide the extra. Decide whether it is a credit toward the next month or something else, and write it down. Check your lease and local rules for how overpayments and deposits should be treated.

Fees and rent are separate

Keep late fees on their own lines, apart from rent. Whether a payment must go first to rent or to fees can depend on your lease and local rules, so check that yourself rather than assuming.

Common mistakes

  • Not writing down the payment method, which causes trouble if a check bounces.
  • Writing a payment against the wrong month.
  • Forgetting to note the promised date for the rest.
  • Entering payments out of order, so the running balance looks wrong.

A quick check each month

For every tenant, add up the charges, add up the payments, and subtract. The result should match your bank records and the balance you tell the tenant.
