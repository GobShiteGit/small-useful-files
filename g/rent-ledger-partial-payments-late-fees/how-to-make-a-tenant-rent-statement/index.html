How do I make a rent statement for a tenant?

A tenant rent statement is a list of everything charged and everything paid over a period, ending with the balance owed. To make one, list each charge with its date, list each payment with its date, add any late fees on their own lines, and subtract payments from charges to reach the balance. Keep it factual and easy to check.

When you need one

  • A tenant asks what they owe.
  • You are sending a reminder and want to show the numbers.
  • There is a disagreement about what was paid.
  • You are closing out a tenancy.

What to include

  • Tenant name, unit, and the period the statement covers.
  • The date you prepared it.
  • Charges: date, description, amount.
  • Payments: date, method, amount.
  • Late fees: the days late, the daily rate, and the amount. Show any part already collected or waived.
  • Balance: the total still owed as of the statement date.

Steps

  1. Choose the period, for example October 1 to November 14.
  2. List every charge in date order.
  3. List every payment in date order.
  4. Add the late fees as separate lines, with how you worked them out.
  5. Add up charges and late fees. Subtract payments and any waived amounts.
  6. Write the result as the balance as of the statement date.
  7. Read it as the tenant would. Could they check each line against their own memory or bank record?

Worked example (made-up numbers)

Statement for Sample Tenant B, as of November 14.

  • Nov 1: Rent for November, charge 1200.00
  • Nov 2: Payment by transfer, 700.00
  • Late fee: grace 3 days, so grace ended Nov 4. Days late to Nov 14: 10. At 5 per day: 50.00

Totals: rent balance 1200 - 700 = 500. Plus late fee 50. Balance as of Nov 14: 550.00.

Note that the late fee will keep growing each day until the rent is paid, so say the date the figure is correct for.

Be clear about the late fee

A late fee only makes sense if your lease supports it and local rules allow it. Rules differ by place, so check yours. Show how the fee was calculated, not just the total. If you waived some of it, show that as a separate line.

Common mistakes

  • No "as of" date, so the balance is unclear.
  • Mixing rent and fees in one number.
  • Leaving out small partial payments.
  • Charges or payments out of date order.
  • Rounding differently from line to line.

Check before you send

Compare the payments on the statement with your bank records. If one is missing, fix your records before the tenant sees the statement.
