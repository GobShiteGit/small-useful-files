How do I prorate rent for a mid-month move-in?

One common method is to multiply the monthly rent by the number of days remaining in the month, counting the move-in day, and divide by the number of days in that month. Your lease may use a different method, so check it before charging anyone.

The formula

Prorated rent = monthly rent x days remaining / days in the month

Days remaining means the days from the move-in date to the end of the month, counting the move-in date itself.

Steps

  1. Find the monthly rent in the lease.
  2. Find how many days are in the move-in month (28, 29, 30 or 31).
  3. Count the days from the move-in date to the last day of the month, including the move-in date.
  4. Multiply the rent by the days remaining, then divide by the days in the month.
  5. Round to cents.
  6. Record the result as its own charge with its own due date and ID.

Worked example 1 (made-up numbers)

Rent is 1000 and the tenant moves in on Nov 16. November has 30 days. From Nov 16 to Nov 30 counting both ends is 15 days.

1000 x 15 / 30 = 500.00

Worked example 2

Rent is 900 and the tenant moves in on Dec 11. December has 31 days. From Dec 11 to Dec 31 is 21 days.

900 x 21 / 31 = 18900 / 31 = 609.68 (rounded to cents)

Counting the days: a quick check

The easy mistake is being off by one. If the move-in is the 16th of a 30-day month, subtracting gives 30 - 16 = 14, but counting the move-in day itself gives 15. Decide whether the move-in day counts, and make sure your lease agrees. The method above counts it.

Move-outs

For a move-out, the days that matter are the days from the start of the month up to the move-out date, not the days after it. Use the days in the month as the divisor, count the days up to and including the move-out date if your lease says the tenant has the unit that day, and adjust by hand. Check the lease for which day counts.

Other methods exist

Some leases use a different approach, such as treating every month as 30 days, or a fixed daily rate written in the agreement. Rules on proration may also depend on where you live. This page is not legal advice. Check your lease and the current local rules yourself, and use whatever method the lease states.

Record it clearly

  • Make the prorated amount its own charge, separate from the first full month.
  • Put the dates and method in the description, for example: Nov 16-30, 15/30 of 1000.
  • Record each payment against that charge, the same way you would for any other.

Common mistakes

  • Using the wrong number of days in the month.
  • Forgetting to count the move-in day, or counting it when the lease does not.
  • Mixing methods between tenants without writing down which one applies.
  • Rounding too early. Calculate the full figure first, then round once at the end.
