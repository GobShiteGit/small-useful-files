How do I calculate a late fee on rent?

One common way: find the day the grace period ends, count the days from then until the rent was fully paid, multiply by the daily fee, and apply a cap if your lease has one. Your lease and local rules decide whether this method is allowed for you, so check them first.

Before you calculate anything

Late fee rules differ by place and by lease. Some places limit the amount or require a minimum grace period, and some leases use a flat fee instead of a daily one. This page does not tell you what is allowed. It shows the arithmetic for one daily-rate method. Read your lease and check the current local rules yourself.

What you need from the lease

  • The due date of the charge.
  • The grace days: days after the due date before a fee starts.
  • The fee per day.
  • The cap, if there is one: the most a single charge can accrue.

Steps

  1. Grace end date = due date + grace days.
  2. Compare the dates. If the charge was fully paid on or before the grace end date, there is no fee. If it is still unpaid, use today's date as the end of the count.
  3. Days late = the date it was fully paid (or today) minus the grace end date. Never go below 0.
  4. Late fee = days late x fee per day.
  5. Apply the cap. If the result is above the cap, use the cap.
  6. Subtract what you already collected or waived to get the fee still outstanding.

Worked example: paid in two parts (made-up numbers)

Rent of 1000 is due Oct 1. Grace is 5 days, the fee is 10 per day, the cap is 100. The tenant pays 600 on Oct 10 and 400 on Oct 20.

  • Grace ends Oct 6.
  • The rent was fully paid on Oct 20 (the date the balance reached zero), so days late = 14.
  • 14 x 10 = 140, which is over the cap, so the fee is 100.

Note that this method measures to the date the charge was fully paid. It does not charge per dollar and it does not compound.

Worked example: still unpaid

Rent of 1000 is due Nov 1. Same terms. Nothing has been paid and today is Nov 14.

  • Grace ends Nov 6.
  • Days late = Nov 14 minus Nov 6 = 8.
  • 8 x 10 = 80, under the cap.
  • Total owed today = 1000 rent + 80 fee = 1080.

The fee keeps growing each day until the rent is paid or the cap is reached.

Worked example: some fee already collected

Rent of 800 is due Oct 1, grace 5 days, fee 8 per day, cap 50. Paid in full on Oct 12. Grace ended Oct 6, so 6 days late, and 6 x 8 = 48. If you collected the 48, the fee outstanding is 0. If you chose to waive it, record it as waived so it does not show as owed.

Common mistakes

  • Counting from the due date instead of the grace end date.
  • Forgetting that a partial payment may not stop the clock. Under this method the charge counts as late until it is fully paid. Your lease may treat it differently.
  • Forgetting the cap.
  • Not recording fees you collected or waived, so old fees keep showing as owed.
  • Applying a rule you assumed rather than one you checked.
